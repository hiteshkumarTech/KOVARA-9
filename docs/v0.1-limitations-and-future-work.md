# KOVARA-9 v0.1 limitations and future work

## Evidence boundary

The v0.1 evidence supports one narrow conclusion: training changed partial exploration and recovery
behavior relative to exact initialization, but did not produce full cooperative task completion.
All three frozen trained policies had zero held-out success and remained below random and frontier
baselines. This document preserves that boundary while identifying testable future hypotheses.

## Scientific limitations

### Small training-seed sample

Only three independent training seeds were used. Their consistency supports a reproducible
descriptive pattern, but not a statistical-significance claim or a complete characterization of PPO
variance.

### Limited held-out distributions

The final evaluation used one 100-seed subset and two structural presets. That is stronger than
validation-only reporting but remains a small sample of possible maps, team sizes, horizons, target
counts, and communication regimes.

### Sparse shared reward

All agents receive a sparse team reward. Partial exploration may not receive timely, agent-specific
credit, and early updates can favor simple action preferences before successful trajectories appear.
Day 7 showed that simply doubling target-recovery reward did not fix this and instead regressed
partial metrics.

### Feed-forward decentralized actor

The actor cannot retain observation history. A single local frame and latest message may be
insufficient for remembering explored regions, maintaining roles, or reasoning about teammate intent.

### Simplified MAPPO variant

The implementation intentionally omits recurrence, value clipping, target-KL early stopping,
distributed collection, population-based methods, and alternative learning algorithms. v0.1 tests
one controlled foundation, not the full MARL design space.

### Deterministic evaluation rule

Masked argmax makes deployment behavior reproducible, but it examines one deterministic projection
of the learned distribution. Stochastic rollouts could behave differently and would require their
own preregistered evaluation question.

### Baseline replication

Random and frontier used the same explicit episode seeds as every learned policy, but were not given
an additional independent policy-seed replication layer. The frontier result is a handcrafted
algorithmic reference, not a learned comparison.

### Simulator abstraction

The grid omits sensing noise, continuous control, dynamics, damage, people, real communication,
uncertain goals, and safety constraints. Simulator behavior must not be extrapolated to physical
rescue.

## Engineering limitations

- CPU-only synchronous collection limits scale.
- The actor/critic MLPs assume fixed encoded signatures.
- Checkpoints are trusted PyTorch binary inputs and are intentionally excluded from Git.
- Structural inference timing was not serialized in the final v0.1 artifact format.
- The recruiter demo is PowerShell-first, although all substantive Python CLI commands are portable.
- GitHub repository rename, PR integration, tag creation, and release publication remain
  owner-managed operational steps documented by the Day 10 release checklist.

## Controlled future-work hypotheses

These are research proposals, not claims that a technique will solve the task.

### Better credit assignment

Hypothesis: decomposed or difference-style learning signals could associate local decisions with
team progress more effectively than the current sparse shared reward. A future study must predefine
one intervention, retain the v0.1 reward as control, and check for reward hacking.

### Curriculum design

Hypothesis: staged target counts, map sizes, or horizons could expose successful trajectories before
the full task. A curriculum must be declared before training and evaluated on an untouched full-task
distribution, not only its easier stages.

### Recurrent memory

Hypothesis: a recurrent actor could preserve exploration history and local role context. This is an
architecture change and requires a new checkpoint schema, resume-equivalence tests, and a new
candidate-selection protocol.

### Explicit coordination mechanisms

Hypothesis: structured communication, role assignment, or permutation-aware teammate modeling could
reduce blocking and improve discovery-to-recovery conversion. Any mechanism must preserve
decentralized execution and prevent centralized information leakage.

### Broader evaluation

Future studies should use more training seeds, multiple untouched structural families, separately
seeded stochastic baselines, and predeclared intervals. A new final partition must be generated and
locked independently; the v0.1 consumed partition must not be reused for tuning or debugging.

## What remains frozen

The v0.1 candidate, reward, architecture, checkpoint selection, final report, and consumed test
partition remain historical scientific records. Future experiments must use new configuration names
and explicit protocol/version boundaries rather than rewriting the v0.1 result.
